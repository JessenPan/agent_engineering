### Agent Evaluation Design

- For customer-service and other tool-using Agents, define each task's customer goal, initial business state, applicable policy, and acceptance criteria before running trials. Reset conversation and business state between trials. Historical conversations inform scenarios; allow different valid paths instead of requiring verbatim replay or one exact tool sequence.
- Keep hidden grading criteria and reference answers out of the Agent and customer simulator's inputs. Give each participant only the information its role permits, including authorized business tool results; do not expose the Agent's private tool results to the simulator. The simulator generates customer behavior; independent graders determine success. Add customer-side tools only when the task requires customer actions.
- Record trial validity, business outcome, policy compliance, and communication quality separately. Prefer executable checks of actual business outcomes when available. Check identity, permission, required consent, and action ordering at the time of the action; a correct final state can still result from an unauthorized action. Quality scores cannot compensate for a failed business or policy requirement. Correct refusal or escalation can count as success when defined in the task criteria.
- Separate test-environment or simulator faults from Agent failures. Agent-caused timeouts, loops, and incorrect actions remain failures. Validity and scoring status are distinct: an otherwise valid trial can remain unscored or uncertain. Report all attempts, missing trials, invalid trials, and unresolved judgments with reasons and explicit denominator rules.
- Define task pass rates, serious-violation rates, communication scores, evaluator errors, latency, and cost separately. Report task groups, sample sizes, uncertainty, and relevant distributions. Include simulator and grading costs alongside Agent execution costs; do not hide critical failures in one average score.
- Distinguish `pass^k` (all k independent trials of the same task succeed under fixed conditions) from `pass@k` (at least one succeeds). Plan n >= k trials per task, state the estimator and aggregation, and do not raise a pooled pass rate to the kth power. Missing or invalid repeats must not be presented as a complete reliability result.
- Calibrate model graders against independent human-reviewed examples using explicit rubrics and relevant conversation and business evidence. Check order and verbosity biases, disagreements, unknown judgments, and especially serious failures incorrectly marked as passes. Human agreement on a benchmark does not establish business accuracy for this project.
- Keep development, grader-calibration, and held-out evaluation data separate; variants of one original case belong in the same split. Record when held-out cases are used for development and replenish independent evaluation data. Compare baseline and candidate under the same evaluation configuration except for the intended change; record task/data splits, state, policies, tools, simulator, rubric, grader, and Agent versions. Re-evaluate comparable samples when scoring changes instead of combining incompatible scores.
- Agree release criteria and serious-violation handling before examining results. After release, link sampled scores to execution evidence, actual business outcomes, user feedback, sampling coverage, and feedback delays. Use reviewed failures for repair and regression; use an appropriate experimental design, such as randomized A/B testing, for causal improvement claims.
- Supplement the references above with [τ²-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07982) for simulator validity and customer actions, [G-Eval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16634) for explicit scoring rubrics, and [Anthropic's Demystifying evals for AI agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/demystifying-evals-for-ai-agents) for task/trial/trace/grader distinctions and evaluation practice. Check applicable versions and limitations before applying them; these sources do not validate the complete proposed architecture.
